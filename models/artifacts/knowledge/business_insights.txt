CROSS-CUTTING BUSINESS INSIGHTS
================================================================
Synthesized "so what" findings that tie the KPI, product, customer,
delivery, sentiment, forecasting, and recommendation analyses together.
This is the primary grounding file for executive summaries and the
GenAI chatbot's "what should we do" style questions.

TOP 10 EDA INSIGHTS (from the master notebook's summary table)
1. ~8% of orders are late; late orders average ~2.2 stars vs ~4.3 stars
   on time -> feeds delay prediction and sentiment modeling.
2. Median delivery is ~10-12 days, but estimated delivery windows are
   conservatively set ~11 days later than typical actual delivery
   -> feeds the logistics dashboard; there's room to tighten promised
   delivery dates without hurting on-time rate, which could raise
   customer trust/perceived speed.
3. Peak ordering happens Monday-Wednesday, 10:00-16:00 -> feeds sales-
   forecasting calendar features and staffing/inventory planning.
4. ~90% of orders are single-item -> the recommendation engine is the
   main cross-sell lever available (see recommendation_results.txt).
5. Only ~3.1% of customers are repeat buyers -> retention/re-engagement
   is flagged as the single biggest growth opportunity (see
   customer_analysis.txt for RFM segment sizing).
6. Credit card is ~74% of payment revenue; ~50% of orders use
   installments -> payment financing options are core to purchase
   behavior, not a marginal feature.
7. Top 20% of products generate 80%+ of revenue (Pareto pattern)
   -> concentration risk if top SKUs go out of stock or get delisted;
   also focuses which products matter most for review-score and
   forecasting accuracy.
8. Product weight strongly drives freight cost (r ≈ 0.66) -> useful for
   freight-cost prediction and packaging/logistics optimization.
9. Negative reviews are longer and more frequent commenters than
   positive ones -> justifies investing in transformer-based (BERTimbau)
   sentiment/complaint mining over review text, not just star ratings.
10. SP/RJ/MG dominate both revenue and customer count -> geographic
    concentration should inform regional logistics/warehouse strategy.

REVENUE CONCENTRATION RISK
- SP alone is ~38% of total revenue; SP+RJ+MG together dominate.
- Top 10 product categories (of 71) generate roughly 60%+ of total
  category revenue.
Both patterns mean the business is exposed to concentration risk (a
single state's economic conditions or a handful of categories'
performance can swing headline KPIs) — worth flagging when reporting
"strong" revenue growth, since it may be driven by a narrow base.

CUSTOMER LIFECYCLE STORY
Retention (3.1% repeat rate) is the biggest gap versus acquisition. RFM
segmentation shows Loyal + Champions customers (57% of the base) drive
~77% of revenue, while "At Risk" customers (34% of the base, avg spend
R$127) represent the largest addressable win-back opportunity by sheer
count. Combined with the finding that on-time seller dispatch correlates
with a 2.4x higher 90-day repeat-purchase rate, a natural high-leverage
recommendation is: tighten seller SLA/dispatch performance first (it's
operationally controllable and has a measured link to repeat purchases),
then layer targeted win-back campaigns for At-Risk RFM segments.

DELIVERY IS THE #1 CONTROLLABLE SATISFACTION LEVER
Across independent analyses (EDA correlation, the review-score
classifier's feature importances, and the sentiment comment-length
finding), delivery timing surfaces as the top or near-top driver of
customer satisfaction every time:
- delivery_vs_estimated and delivery_days are the #1 and #2 most
  important features in the Random Forest review-score classifier
  (86.69% test accuracy).
- Average delivery time drops monotonically from 19.1 days (1-star) to
  10.2 days (5-star) reviews.
- Late orders average ~2.2 stars vs ~4.3 for on-time.
This convergence across three independently trained models/analyses
makes "reduce delivery delay/variance" the platform's highest-confidence
lever for improving customer satisfaction and, transitively, repeat
purchase rate.

FORECASTING RELIABILITY CAVEAT
The best sales-forecasting models (XGBoost/RandomForest) explain only
~37-38% of daily GMV variance (R2 ≈ 0.37) on the holdout, with ~21%
weighted absolute percentage error. Forecasts should be presented as
directional/trend guidance (and are notably useful for Black Friday /
holiday season magnitude estimates) rather than precise day-level
revenue commitments. Classical models (Prophet, ARIMA, LSTM) performed
worse than the tree-based models and are not recommended as the primary
forecast source for this dataset.

MODEL PORTFOLIO SUMMARY (which model answers which question)
- "How happy are our customers overall / by category / by state?"
  -> sentiment_analysis.txt (BERTimbau text sentiment, 85.84% accuracy;
     or the structured review-score classifier, 86.69% accuracy)
- "What will revenue look like next quarter / around Black Friday?"
  -> forecasting_results.txt (XGBoost/RandomForest, R2 ≈ 0.37-0.38)
- "What should we recommend to a customer viewing product X?"
  -> recommendation_results.txt (content-based KNN or SVD-based KNN)
- "Where is revenue/growth concentrated and who are our best customers?"
  -> sales_kpis.txt + customer_analysis.txt (RFM segmentation)
- "Why is a specific order/customer/product underperforming?"
  -> delivery_analysis.txt + product_analysis.txt (delivery delay,
     freight cost, category-level patterns)
