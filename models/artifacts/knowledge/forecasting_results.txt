SALES / GMV FORECASTING RESULTS
================================================================
Goal: forecast daily Gross Merchandise Value (GMV = daily payment_value)
so the platform can answer "what will revenue look like over the next
90 days," including around Black Friday and the December holiday season.

PIPELINE
1. Encode seasonal + category/state features (SalesFeatureEncoder).
2. Aggregate the 119,143 order-item rows into a DAILY GMV PANEL
   (TSAggregator) — one row per calendar day of revenue.
3. Engineer lag features (lag_1, lag_2, lag_3, lag_7, lag_14, lag_21,
   lag_30) and rolling-window stats (roll_mean/roll_std over 7/14/30
   days) plus calendar features (dayofweek, week, month, dayofyear,
   quarter, year, is_weekend, is_black_friday, is_holiday_season) and
   Fourier/cyclic encodings (sin/cos of dayofweek, week, month, dayofyear).
4. IMPORTANT DESIGN CHOICE: only features genuinely knowable in advance
   (lags/rolling stats of the target + calendar signals) are used.
   Same-day aggregates like order_volume, shipping_revenue, or
   category/state mix-shares are themselves outputs of that day's orders
   and would leak information a real forecast wouldn't have — these are
   deliberately excluded from FEATURE_COLS.
5. Train/test split: last 30 days (train_forecasting.py) or a 113-day
   holdout (exploratory notebook) held out chronologically — no shuffling
   (TimeSeriesSplit used for CV during tuning).

TAKEAWAYS
- Tree-based gradient boosting models (XGBoost, RandomForest, LightGBM)
  clearly beat classical time-series methods (Prophet, ARIMA) and a
  simple LSTM on this daily GMV series — the engineered lag/rolling/
  calendar features give trees enough signal to outperform models that
  rely mostly on trend/seasonality decomposition.
- XGBoost and RandomForest are close (WAPE 21.06% vs 21.42%, R2 0.375
  vs 0.374) — either is a defensible production choice; XGBoost is
  marginally best on the holdout, RandomForest wins on the sample
  91-day out-of-sample retrain-and-run comparison in train_forecasting.py.
- Prophet/ARIMA/LSTM all post negative R2 on this holdout, meaning they
  perform worse than simply predicting the mean daily GMV — daily GMV
  for this dataset is noisy/spiky (promotions, Black Friday) in a way
  classical decomposition methods don't capture well without more
  exogenous regressors.
- R2 in the 0.31-0.38 range for the best models indicates daily GMV
  is genuinely hard to forecast precisely from calendar + lag features
  alone — useful for trend/magnitude, less reliable for exact day-level
  numbers, and should be communicated with that caveat.

HYPERPARAMETER TUNING (XGBoost)
RandomizedSearchCV, 50 candidates, TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=3),
scoring='neg_mean_absolute_error'. Best params found: subsample=0.6,
reg_lambda=1.5, reg_alpha=10, n_estimators=400, min_child_weight=1,
max_depth=3, learning_rate=0.08, gamma=0.5, colsample_bytree=0.9
(best CV score: MAE ≈ 9,418.74).
Counter-intuitive result: the TUNED XGBoost performed WORSE than the
default/untuned XGBoost on the actual holdout — e.g. on the last 5 test
days, untuned XGBoost error ranged from -9.7% to -41.0% vs tuned
XGBoost's -4.7% to -33.1% (mixed, but on balance the notebook concludes
"normal XGBoost model is predicting better than the tuned one" for this
particular holdout window). This is flagged explicitly as a real
finding, not a bug — cross-validated MAE improvement did not translate
to holdout improvement here, likely because the randomized search
optimized a shallower/more regularized tree (max_depth 3, high L1
penalty reg_alpha=10) that generalizes to typical days but under-reacts
to the extreme demand spikes (Black Friday, promo days) that dominate
this particular holdout window.

FEATURE IMPORTANCE (XGBoost)
Lag features (especially recent lags: lag_1, lag_7) and rolling means
dominate feature importance, alongside calendar signals — see the
"Top 15 Predictive Feature Importances" chart in the notebook for the
full ranking.

PRODUCTION ARTIFACTS (models/artifacts/, written by train_forecasting.py)
- trained_forecasting_models.pkl — dict of all trained models
  (RandomForest, LGBM at minimum; Hybrid commented out in current script)
- best_forecasting_model.pkl — winning model retrained on FULL history
- daily_gmv_history.csv — the daily GMV panel used for training
- future_forecast_90d.csv — 90-day-ahead forecast, generated by
  iteratively rolling forward day-by-day and regenerating lag/rolling
  features from prior actual + predicted daily_gmv values (so later days
  in the 90-day horizon are increasingly dependent on the model's own
  earlier predictions)
- forecasting_metadata.json — best_model name, trained_at timestamp,
  history date range, evaluation metrics, and the exact feature_cols list

BLACK FRIDAY / HOLIDAY SEASON
is_black_friday (November, day >= 22) and is_holiday_season (December,
day <= 24) are explicit binary flags carried through both training and
the 90-day future forecast, so the dashboard can specifically show
projected Black Friday and December holiday GMV uplifts across all
compared models.
