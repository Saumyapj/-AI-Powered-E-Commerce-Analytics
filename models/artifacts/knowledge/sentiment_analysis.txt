SENTIMENT ANALYSIS & CUSTOMER SATISFACTION MODELS
================================================================
NOTE: there are two related but distinct modeling efforts in this
project — (A) text-based sentiment classification from Portuguese
review comments, and (B) a structured-features review-score (1-5)
classifier that powers customer_satisfaction_app.py. Both are
documented here since they answer "how happy are our customers" from
different angles.

REVIEW SCORE / SENTIMENT LABEL DISTRIBUTION (118,146 labeled reviews)
- 5 stars: 66,343 (56.2%)
- 4 stars: 22,319 (18.9%)
- 3 stars: 9,894 (8.4%)
- 2 stars: 4,162 (3.5%)
- 1 star: 15,428 (13.1%)
Sentiment bucket (<=2 Negative, ==3 Neutral, >=4 Positive):
- Positive: 89,659 (~75%)
- Negative: 19,590 (~16-17%)
- Neutral: 9,894 (~8%)
Score distribution is bimodal: customers overwhelmingly leave either 5
stars or 1 star, rarely 2-3 — reviews are driven by strong sentiment in
either direction more than lukewarm middle-ground feedback.

COMMENT BEHAVIOR BY SENTIMENT
- Negative reviews: 100% have a comment, average comment length 88.4
  characters (longest).
- Neutral reviews: 100% have a comment, average length 53.0 characters.
- Positive reviews: 100% have a comment, average length 33.8 characters
  (shortest).
Unhappy customers write more and longer comments — a key justification
for investing in a transformer-based (BERTimbau) sentiment/complaint-
mining model rather than relying on star rating alone.

(A) TEXT-BASED SENTIMENT MODELS (Negative / Neutral / Positive from
review_comment_title + review_comment_message)
Two models were trained and compared on a held-out test set of 23,630
reviews:

1. Baseline: TF-IDF (max_features=5000) + Logistic Regression
   - Accuracy: 85.01%
   - Negative: precision 0.7812, recall 0.6751, f1 0.7243 (support 3,918)
   - Neutral:  precision 0.5200, recall 0.0526, f1 0.0955 (support 1,979)
   - Positive: precision 0.8650, recall 0.9778, f1 0.9180 (support 17,733)
   - Macro avg f1: 0.5792; weighted avg f1: 0.8170

2. BERTimbau (neuralmind/bert-base-portuguese-cased, fine-tuned,
   2 epochs, batch size 32)
   - Accuracy: 85.84% (best model)
   - Negative: precision 0.8274, recall 0.6792, f1 0.7460
   - Neutral:  precision 0.4786, recall 0.1243, f1 0.1974
   - Positive: precision 0.8732, recall 0.9799, f1 0.9235
   - Macro avg f1: 0.6223; weighted avg f1: 0.8333

Both models are strong on Positive and reasonable on Negative, but both
struggle badly on Neutral (recall 5-12%) — the middle sentiment class is
genuinely hard to separate from mild positive/negative language, and
Neutral reviews are a small minority (1,979 of 23,630 test rows).
BERTimbau outperforms the TF-IDF baseline on every class, most notably
on Negative precision (0.827 vs 0.781) and overall macro-F1 (0.622 vs
0.579), justifying the heavier transformer as the production sentiment
model when compute allows; the TF-IDF+LogReg baseline (tfidf_vectorizer.pkl
+ sentiment_model.pkl) remains the lightweight fallback served by
train_sentiment.py when torch/transformers aren't available.

(B) CUSTOMER SATISFACTION / REVIEW-SCORE (1-5) CLASSIFIER
This is the model behind customer_satisfaction_app.py. Unlike the text
model above, it predicts the exact 1-5 review_score from 23 structured
order/product/payment/delivery features (order_status, price,
freight_value, product attributes, payment attributes, delivery_days,
delivery_vs_estimated, approval_hours, etc.), NOT from review text.

Pipeline: label encoding (categoricals) -> StandardScaler -> outlier
removal (IQR) -> SMOTE class balancing (207,435 balanced training rows,
~33,187 per class) -> 80/20 train/test split, stratified.

Models compared (test accuracy on 41,484 held-out rows):
- Random Forest (RandomizedSearchCV, 20 candidates x 3-fold CV):
  best params {n_estimators=100, min_samples_split=5, min_samples_leaf=1,
  max_features='sqrt', max_depth=20, criterion='entropy'};
  CV score 0.8433; TEST ACCURACY 86.69% (best overall model)
  weighted precision 0.879, recall 0.867, f1 0.869
- Decision Tree (15 candidates x 3-fold CV): best params
  {splitter='best', min_samples_split=5, min_samples_leaf=1, max_depth=20,
  criterion='gini'}; CV score 0.5797; TEST ACCURACY 59.99%
  weighted precision 0.636, recall 0.600, f1 0.601
- XGBoost (20 candidates x 3-fold CV): best params {subsample=0.8,
  n_estimators=150, min_child_weight=1, max_depth=7, learning_rate=0.2,
  colsample_bytree=0.9}; CV score 0.7753; TEST ACCURACY 78.70%
  weighted precision 0.802, recall 0.787, f1 0.785

Best model: Random Forest (86.69% test accuracy), saved as
best_sentiment_model.pkl alongside random_forest_best.pkl,
decision_tree_best.pkl, xgboost_best.pkl, scaler.pkl, and
label_encoders.pkl — these are exactly the files customer_satisfaction_app.py
loads from models/.

Top 10 feature importances (Random Forest): delivery_vs_estimated (0.083),
delivery_days (0.079), customer_zip_code_prefix (0.056), payment_value
(0.054), approval_hours (0.053), price (0.051), freight_value (0.051),
customer_city (0.050), product_description_lenght (0.049),
product_name_lenght (0.047). Delivery timing dominates the top of the
list, consistent with delivery_analysis.txt.

On a 10-sample spot check, Random Forest got 8/10 correct, Decision Tree
6/10, XGBoost only 1/10 — reinforcing Random Forest as the production
choice despite XGBoost's stronger CV/test numbers in the sales-forecasting
task elsewhere in the platform (model choice is task-specific, not a
universal ranking).

SENTIMENT BY DELIVERY STATUS / CATEGORY / STATE
sentiment_metadata.json (produced by train_sentiment.py) additionally
stores sentiment_by_delivery_status, sentiment_by_top_category (for the
10 highest-review-volume categories: bed_bath_table, health_beauty,
sports_leisure, furniture_decor, computers_accessories, housewares,
watches_gifts, telephony, garden_tools, auto), and sentiment_by_state_top10
crosstabs, used to power the drill-down explorer's sentiment views.
